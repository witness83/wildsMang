You are a hunter-gatherer

The old world is gone. The jungle took it back. All you keep is one road to walk and whatever you can pick up along it.

Gear on the ground is yours the moment you step on it — no menus, no bags. Off-hand, head, chest, legs, boots, gloves, belt, two trinkets: nine slots, dropped straight off the beasts.

One rule matters more than the rest:
find the exact same piece again, and it evolves on the spot.
White, blue, purple, gold, then red.

That is how a named piece gets made — you pick it up, piece by piece. Nobody hands it to you. So no duplicate is junk.

How it plays

One hand is enough

By default you auto-target and auto-swing; all you do is move — dodge the bites, slip the lunges, and plant yourself in the thickest crowd for XP. Want the rhythm in your own hands? Switch to manual: left stick to move, a button in the corner to strike. Hold it and you keep cutting.

It only gets worse

No timer, no quota to clear. Every wave is thicker than the last, every formation nastier. Clear it and a bigger one comes. The wave you can't clear is the end of your run.

Break a boss, open a shop

Every time you bring down a boss, a supply point lights up on the map. Spend the fireflies you've gathered on gear. The first one always holds a signature weapon for your character — and it rewrites how you fight.

Two currencies, both count

Fireflies die with the run. Coins come home, sit by the campfire, and buy you new characters, new maps, and one small permanent nudge at a time. Next run, you last longer.

Two worlds

Daywild is open sunlit plains and pack hunters. Nightwild is a moon-blue deep wood — creeks, mimicry, luring calls, everything half-hidden. Even the loot changes: beast-bone blades and throwing spears on one side, totem branches and glowing runestones on the other.

Four characters, four ways to live and die

The Hunter is steady. The Whetstone is slow but won't fold. The Shadewalker is brittle and fast. The Totemancer lays fire in circles. Same rules, different hands.

The Roster

Hunter

Balanced, attack-leaning. Clean swings, short recovery. The easiest place to start.

Whetstone

Heavy and patient. Slow swings, wide reach, and enough bulk to stand in the middle of it.

Shadewalker

Brittle and fast, built around the bow. Hit, slip away, hit again — never trade blows.

Totemancer

Casts from range with the totem staff and burns circles into the ground around itself.

No story to chase. No daily chores.
A run is twenty, thirty minutes — and when you go down, you take another life.

What you carry out was never the loot.
It was whether you make it back to the fire.
